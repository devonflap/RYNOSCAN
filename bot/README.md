# RynoBot (dry run)

Seven always-on bots that do what a trading bot would do, except place orders until you arm them. Each runs as its own service with its own $10,000 paper account and kitty, and the **bot desk** (below) shows all seven on one page. Three of them (lab, minute, trench) are **research bots that can never go live** (`labOnly`: live is refused whatever the config says); they exist to answer questions faster.

- **runner** (`rynobot@runner`): the degen bot. The runner of the day, hunted **every minute**: the scanner's ~80-coin universe is quoted on DexScreener each cycle and a coin whose last hour's volume is 2.5x its 24-hour pace, with buyers 1.15x sellers, up 3-60% in the hour and not already up 150% in a day, is bought on sight (2 new a minute at most, one trade per coin per 72 hours, $50k+ liquidity, the 2-hour survivor gate, no wallet or holder vetoes and no score: the pure test). **Scale-out (23 Oct):** 20% out at +100%, +200%, +300% and +400%; the last 20% is a **trailing take-profit**, sold 35% off its high, for as long as it runs. **Stop (23 Oct):** 2% under the lows of the last two closed hourly candles, kept between 12% and 30% from the entry (the coin's own structure, not a flat 20%; 20% when candles are missing). **Out early** when sellers are dumping it (5-minute sells 1.5x buys and the hour 1.2x, 15% off the high), when half of a 30%+ gain is given back (the **HOOKR rule**, see below), on the seller cut (8%+ under entry with sellers in control) or when buyers stop following for a day.
- **breakout** (`rynobot@breakout`): Breakout retest, see **The Breakout bot** further down. The one rulebook with positive closed trades so far; unchanged but for the HOOKR giveback rule below (its paper twin keeps the old exits, so the weekly reality check shows what the rule costs or saves). Not risking more yet: 2 closed trades is no basis, and the council's size study answers the sizing question at 30.
- **rider** (`rynobot@rider`): the giant catcher, built from the giants study. Not the launch: **the first real pullback on day 2-7** of a coin that has already proven it can move (1.5x+ its first day's close), 20-45% off its running high, the whole pool changing hands daily (24-hour volume 1.5x the liquidity, $150k+), buyers at least level in the hour, bought when the last closed hourly candle turns green above the previous close. Candidates are the scanner's universe plus GeckoTerminal's trending pools (two pages every 10 minutes). Stop 5% under the pullback low (15-35%). Clips 15% at 2x, 3x, 4x and 5x; **the last 40% is the giant ticket: no stop, no giveback rule, no dump cut** (a giant's dips would shake it out), trailing 55% off its high until the giants study has run, then the study's answer (its median worst dip before the top plus 10 points, kept between 45% and 70%; the bot reads `docs/data/giants.json` hourly). Up to 3 entries per coin a day apart, because a giant gives several pullbacks. Out if no new high in 3 days while under entry; 60 days at most. 0.5% risk a trade, 5% of the pot at most. The council slices its trades by entry day (1.5-2.5, 2.5-4, 4-7), pullback depth and first-or-later entry, so the window tightens from data.
- **btc** and **eth** (retired 24 Oct: no edge in any of eight readings of the rules over a year of BTC and ETH, longs or shorts; the rules as written lost -0.47R a trade over 89; the installer stops and masks them, their files are kept): CSK's Monday-range trade, as written, on the majors, on the **UTC Monday** (00:00-24:00 UTC; `rangeTz: 'America/New_York'` switches to New York's Monday, clock changes handled, and the backtest runs both so the data says which works). Tuesday to Sunday: the latest sweep of Monday's low (long) or high (short), an hourly close back inside, a 15-minute structure break and the order block it leaves; a limit at the block's edge for 24 hours, filled at its own price; stop just beyond the block's body; everything out at the other side of Monday's range; 3R to 8R only; 5 days at most. The short side is the long detector run on the price turned upside down, so the two sides are exact mirrors and can never drift apart.
  **Venue: 10x perps, paper only** (`venue: 'perp'`): USDC collateral, long and short, one of each at most, 1% of the pot at risk each (the stop plus fees), size capped at a quarter of the pot as margin, costs as Jupiter Perps (0.06% each way, borrow hourly on the full size), liquidation modelled (the whole margin), and the stop must sit inside 80% of the way to the liquidation price. Leverage does not make a trade better: it lets a 0.8% stop carry the full 1% risk with a sliver of the pot as margin, where spot capped at half the pot could only risk 0.4%. **There is no perps executor: live is refused for perps** whatever the config says. Building one (Jupiter Perps or Drift) is its own job, after the backtest and a few weeks of paper say the strategy is worth it.
  **Spot, if ever wanted** (`venue: 'spot'`): cbBTC (Coinbase's own BTC on Solana, no bridge) and Wormhole's Portal WETH (the only real ETH on Solana; bridged), **bought with USDC and sold back to USDC** (`baseMint`), so the pot sits in dollars between trades, long only. These issuers keep mint and freeze authority by design, so they are on a named trusted list that skips that one safety check (nothing else is skipped). Safer ETH than Wormhole: perps (no ETH is held at all) or ETH on Robinhood chain through its own rollup bridge, bought with USDC.
  **The backtest** (`src/majorsbt.mjs`, `.github/workflows/majors.yml`, Sundays or Run workflow) walks the bots' own detector hour by hour over a year of Coinbase candles with no look-ahead, plays every order and trade on 15-minute candles with the unkind assumption whenever a candle could go either way, charges perp costs, and reports longs, shorts and both against five rulebooks (as written, New York Monday, discount rule, 2R minimum, previous-day range). On pure random prices both sides score zero, give or take luck. An EDGE verdict needs the luck range above zero and both halves of the year positive. The council reads it each run.
  **Found and fixed while building these (23 Oct, never reached: nothing was live):** the live buy worked out SOL's price as "the pair's dollar price over its price in the quote token", which is SOL's price only for SOL-quoted pairs; for a USDC-quoted pair it is about $1, and a $50 order would have tried to spend 50 SOL. The SOL and ETH prices now come only from a SOL- or WETH-quoted pair, or from their own fresh price fetched every 10 minutes outside dry run; a stale price refuses the order.
- **lab** (`rynobot@lab`, research): the same minute-scan signals as the runner, but **every signal is traded seven ways at once**, each with a seventh of the risk, so one choice is measured at a time against the base case on the same coins: **A-base** (buy now, flat 20% stop, Ryan's old clips), **B-pullback** (wait for the first 12%+ dip and buy the first 5-minute close back up), **C-control** (a **random** other coin from the universe bought at the same moment with the same exits: the luck baseline; if it does as well as A, the entry signal is worth nothing and the exits are doing the work), **D-structure** (the structure stop), **E-cash-early** (50% at +50%, 30% at +100%, the rest trails), **F-momentum** (the ignition-style trail and 90% out), **G-giveback** (the HOOKR rules). The council reads it variant by variant.
- **minute** (`rynobot@minute`, research): does a 1-minute entry and exit trade better? Looser scan (volume 1.8x, buyers 1.05x, up 2-120% in the hour, under 400% in a day, $25k liquidity, no age gate), entry on the first closed green 1-minute candle after the signal, exit on a 1-minute dump candle (down 8%+ on 2x the usual minute volume) at any stage, and once up 20% a stop trailing 2% under the last three minutes' lows; then clips at +50/+100/+200% and the runner's cuts. It will rug sometimes. That is the data.
- **trench** (`rynobot@trench`, research): the **trencher**, a pump.fun "trench extractor" on paper. It listens to the free PumpPortal launch feed (every new pump.fun token and every buy and sell on it; no key, no account), gets in when a launch 30 seconds to 12 minutes old has 12+ different buyers, buys 1.5x sells over the last 2 minutes and 4+ buys this minute, a market cap of 30-150 SOL (still early on the curve), the dev under 8% of the supply and no single wallet over 25% of the buying; $100 a go, flat. Then it sells **into the buyers**: 30% at 1.5x, 30% at 2.2x, 20% at 3.5x, the rest trails 30%; and it dumps the lot the moment sellers take over, half a +25% gain is given back, the chart goes 4 minutes without a trade, 15 minutes pass with no new high while under entry, or an hour is up. Stop 35%. Nothing in it can place an order (pump.fun orders are not even implemented). Its record is the first real read on whether the trenches can be traded by rules.
  **Rebuilt 23 Oct: it never fired.** Every launch aged out at 12 minutes without a dozen buyers, which is what happens when PumpPortal's per-coin trade messages never arrive (launches came through, trades did not). It now has three lanes so no single feed can silence it: the feed lane as above (when trades arrive); **launches judged on DexScreener** when the feed has no trades for them (2-20 minutes old, 25+ buys in 5 minutes and 1.4x the sells, $4k+ traded in 5 minutes, $7k-$80k market cap; checked in batches of 30, each launch at most once a minute); and a **trending lane** for what is hot on pump.fun right now: GeckoTerminal's 5-minute and 1-hour trending pools and new pools on the pump.fun and PumpSwap exchanges, every 2 minutes, judged on DexScreener (under 3 days old, 30+ buys in 5 minutes and 1.3x the sells, $50k+ traded in the hour, up 10-300% in the hour and still rising, $20k-$20m cap, one trade per coin per 6 hours), with a 25% stop, 6 hours at most, out if no new high in 30 minutes while under the entry. (Fomo, the app, has no public data feed; its trending coins are pump.fun and PumpSwap coins and show up in the trending lane when they move.) Once an hour the trencher writes what it saw ("last hour: N launches and M trades from PumpPortal; setups by lane; launches that aged out") so a silent feed shows on the desk, and it alerts once if launches keep coming for an hour with no trades at all. Bonding-curve pairs often carry no liquidity figure on DexScreener; a fifth of the market cap stands in for it, marked as an estimate, and only feeds the cost model.
  **Two ways on every shot (24 Oct).** The first day's real trades (198, after taking out the two that a pricing bug invented) showed why the extractor cannot work as a shotgun: wins averaged +$42 and losses -$41 with 44% winning, because the exits sold early and capped every runner (30 trades doubled, the best reached 6.2x, the median hold was 6 minutes), while the 77 shots that never got 10% up rode the stop down for -$38 each. So every shot is now traded two ways, $100 each, on the same coin at the same moment: **X-extract** (the rules above) and **S-shotgun**, the KOL way: a miss costs little (out if never up 15% in 10 minutes, stop 25%), a hit is never capped (half sold at 2x so the initial is back, the rest a free ride with no stop, no giveback and no dump cut, trailing 50% off its high, dead after 15 quiet minutes, up to 7 days). The council compares them variant by variant. The shotgun only works if the fat tail shows up often enough: roughly one shot in a hundred running 20x or more pays for all the misses. Two weeks of shots will say whether it does.

## Modes: dry, rehearsal, live

`"mode"` in `bot/config.json` (shared, or per bot under `"bots"`):

- **`dry`** (default): paper fills from DexScreener prices. Nothing touches the market.
- **`quote`** (rehearsal): every buy and sell asks the real market for a quote (Jupiter on Solana) and books the **quoted** fill, but sends nothing. This measures the real slippage, routing and price impact for this bot's sizes with zero risk, and runs the same token safety and honeypot checks a live order would. Needs the Solana libraries (`npm install`, done by the installer) and a token address on each setup; no wallet or key.
- **`live`**: sends the swap, waits for confirmation, reads the wallet to confirm what was really bought. Live is refused, and the bot stays in dry run with a phone message saying why, unless **all** of these hold: a wallet address for the bot in config, its key file on the server (`/etc/rynobot/keys/<bot>.json`, owner read only), the file `bot/ARMED-<bot>` containing exactly `I understand this bot will spend real money from its own wallet`, and the caps set (`liveMaxUsd` per order, default $50; `liveMaxOpenUsd` in total, default $300).

**Slippage.** The tolerance sent with an order is not a cost; it is the most the fill may move against you before the chain rejects the trade. Too tight and orders fail (you pay the fee and miss the entry; on a stop that is dangerous), too loose and a sandwich bot takes the difference. So each order sets its own: twice the quote's price impact plus 0.5%, floor 0.5%, capped at `liveSlipPct` (3%) for buys and three times that for exits. The real cost is in the quote itself (impact plus fees), and the order carries a priority fee (capped at 0.002 SOL) so it lands fast. On Robinhood chain the same tolerance goes into the aggregator quote or the router call as the minimum output.

**Robinhood chain.** Built from the October 2026 research: the bot quotes **0x, KyberSwap, 1inch and the Uniswap Trading API in parallel** (about 1.5 seconds), drops any quote whose call target or spender is not on its allowlist (Permit2, Uniswap's Universal Router v2.1.2, SwapRouter02, 0x's AllowanceHolder, KyberSwap's router; 0x's Settler is checked against 0x's on-chain registry and never approved), takes the best output net of gas, and **simulates the transaction** before sending. If every aggregator fails or has no route (common for brand-new tokens) it falls back to a direct Uniswap v3 swap built by the bot itself: QuoterV2 picks the best fee tier, then it encodes **Universal Router v2.1.2** calldata (a buy wraps the ETH it sends and swaps it, so no approval is ever needed on the way in; a sell pulls the token through Permit2, swaps and unwraps back to ETH), simulated like any other order. SwapRouter02 is the fallback to the fallback. Gas is 2x the live base fee with no tip, because Robinhood's single sequencer is first come first served and a tip buys nothing; speed does. Fee-on-transfer tokens are refused. API keys go in `/etc/rynobot.env` as `ZEROX_API_KEY`, `ONEINCH_API_KEY`, `UNISWAP_API_KEY` (KyberSwap needs none; with no keys the bot still has KyberSwap plus the direct fallback). Budget 1c to 50c of gas a swap, and use a paid RPC (`"rhRpc"` in config) before going live: the public one is rate-limited. A bot's wallet is on one chain (`walletChain`), so a live bot trades only coins on its wallet's chain; in rehearsal it quotes both.

Every live or rehearsal order goes through: token safety (no freeze authority, no mint authority), a honeypot check (it must be sellable back for at least half), a wallet balance check (order plus a 0.03 SOL fee reserve), a slippage limit (`liveSlipPct` 3% on buys, 3x that on exits so a stop can still get out), and after a live buy the wallet's token balance is what the position holds. A refused exit keeps the position, tells your phone, and is retried every check. Once an hour in live mode the bot compares the wallet with its books and shouts if they drift. Live works on Solana (Jupiter) and Robinhood chain (the executor above); a bot trades the chain its wallet is on.

## Wallets

One wallet per bot, on one chain per bot, made **on the server**, so neither bot can touch the other's money:

```
node bot/wallet.mjs create --bot rules                      # new Solana wallet; key saved owner-only; address put in bot/config.json
node bot/wallet.mjs create --bot degen --chain robinhood    # the same on Robinhood chain (an Ethereum-style wallet)
node bot/wallet.mjs show --bot rules                        # address and balance (the chain is read from the key file)
node bot/wallet.mjs export --bot rules                      # the secret key, shown once, for a one-time import into Phantom
```

Fund a Solana wallet by sending SOL to the address from Phantom or an exchange, with no more than that bot may lose, plus 0.03 SOL for fees. Fund a Robinhood-chain wallet with ETH on Robinhood chain (withdraw from Robinhood to Robinhood chain, or bridge) and keep 0.002 ETH spare for gas.

**Seeing a bot's wallet in Phantom (one-time export).** Phantom cannot be "connected" to a server (its key stays in your phone and every trade would need a tap), so the bots have their own keys. To watch or withdraw from a bot's wallet by hand, run `export` on the server once and import the key into Phantom: *Add wallet > Import private key*, pick Solana for a Solana bot or Ethereum for a Robinhood-chain bot (the same key and address hold the Robinhood-chain ETH; if the app does not list Robinhood chain yet, MetaMask or Rabby show it after adding the network, which `show` prints: chain id 4663, RPC `https://rpc.mainnet.chain.robinhood.com`). Then clear the screen and the shell history (`history -c`). Never paste a Phantom key into the server or a chat; the bots never need your main wallet.

On the desk each bot wears its chain's colour (Solana purple, Robinhood green, BNB yellow, Base blue, Ethereum blue-grey): once a wallet is set the bot trades only that chain, so the colour tells you at a glance which money each bot is on. Until then the colour follows the chain most of its paper trades are on.

## Publishing to the scanner (lets the council argue over the bots)

With `"publish": true` in config and a GitHub token in `/etc/rynobot.env` (`GITHUB_TOKEN=`; a fine-grained token on the RYNOSCAN repo only, Contents: read and write), each bot writes its record to `docs/data/bots/<bot>.json` once an hour: trades with their pre- and post-entry facts, passed-on setups, refused orders. The council's **Bot desk** hat reads these every scan: it compares each bot with its paper twin (runner with Risky · Ryan, breakout with Breakout bot filters), reports fills against the trigger price, refused orders and how often saying no was right, and once a bot has 10+ closed trades it slices them by the bot's own gates (stop distance, hour, chain, score, reward to risk, ignition volume, 24-hour change, liquidity, price impact) and argues for a filter wherever one bucket beats another by 0.5R or more. Nothing private is published (no wallet, no balances, no keys).

The same trigger and exits are also replayed on the dashboard's **Bot rules** tab (5-minute entries, 15-minute exits), next to a plain zone limit and the study exits on the same setups, so the bot's rules are always measured against the alternatives.

## The Breakout bot

Breakout retest exactly as the backtest traded it, because that is what the +0.93R record measured:

- **Setups:** every Breakout retest the scanner sees with a valid plan (score 50+, $100k+ liquidity, no rug danger, not already through its stop), bid-ready or not, from `breakouts.json`. One per coin per 24 hours.
- **Filters (the bot tuner):** it starts with one, reward to risk to TP2 of 3R or more (the biggest and most sensible hint: 24 of the 37 trades, +1.41R against -0.10R for 2-3R). Stops up to 35% are allowed (the widest stops did best; the rules bot's 20% cap would have blocked them). Every other hint from the pattern analyst is tested on new trades and switches on by itself once it clearly holds there (10+ trades kept, 8+ removed, 0.3R+ better, and a luck check); a filter that new trades clearly contradict switches off. You get one phone note when that happens. The tuner never changes sizing, stops or targets.
- **Entry:** a limit order at the top of the retest zone, 48 hours to fill; cancelled if the price falls through the stop first.
- **Exits:** the pattern plan's stop; a third at TP1 and the stop to break-even; a third at TP2; the rest at TP3; out after 7 days.
- **Sizing:** the same as the rules bot: 1% of its paper account lost at the stop (slippage included), 4 positions, 4% total risk, no new buys after a 3% losing day.
- **Its paper twin** is the dashboard's **Breakout → Bot filters** book, and its reality check compares against that.

## The bot desk (a web page on the server)

Open `http://<server address>:8080` (or `https://<your domain>` once HTTPS is set up: `bash bot/install.sh --https desk.yourdomain.com`) on your computer or phone, any user name plus the desk password you chose at install. **Overview** at the top: combined equity with its curve, today, average a day, open positions, risk in use and the record; every bot's account curve on one chart; bot profit (banked and open) as bars; where the money is (cash and positions by bot); then the **Active bots** table (chain, mode, equity, total P&L, average a day, banked, open, record, status). Below it the bot cards side by side: paper account and its curve with each day's result, what each bot is watching and how far the nearest setup is from its zone, risk in use, today's result, the record, open positions with live P&L in dollars and R, the watch list, closed trades, and what the bots did. Click any position, trade or watched setup to see it on a 5-minute chart with the entry, stop, targets and every buy and sell, next to the rules it was taken under. It refreshes itself every 15 seconds.

Also on it: a ticker of everything held and watched, each open position's stop-to-target bar, the win or loss streak with the average win and loss, **Passed on** (every setup the bot said no to, followed for 24 hours: what it did since, its best price, and whether its stop would have hit, so you can see how often saying no was right), and the latest reality check.

**Controls on the desk** need the **separate control password** chosen at install (asked once per browser tab) plus a confirm click: **Close now** on an open position (the bot sells everything left at the live price at its next check, marked "by hand"), **Cancel** on a watched setup, and **Pause / Resume new buys** per bot (open positions are still managed while paused). With no control password set the desk is **read only** and the buttons are greyed out; `DASH_CONTROLS=local` in `/etc/rynobot-desk.env` allows them only from the server itself (SSH tunnel). Nothing else on the page can change anything: no rules, no sizing, no keys. See **bot/SECURITY.md**.

**Charts.** Click any position, trade or watched setup: the **live chart** is DexScreener's own, embedded (fast, always current, with its indicators), and beside it a **ladder** draws the trade's levels on one price scale (entry, stops, targets, every buy ▲ and sell ▼, and the price now). **Our candles + marks** switches to the bot's own 5-minute candles with the entry, stop, targets and fills drawn on the chart (fetched by the server, cached; slower). Your choice is remembered.

**Wallets.** Each bot gets its own wallet, so neither can touch the other's money; that is the lock-off. Only the public address goes in `bot/config.json`: `"bots": { "rules": { "wallet": "<address>", "walletChain": "solana" } }` (chains: solana, bsc, base, ethereum). The desk then shows the balance, read from the chain, with a link to the explorer. The private key is never stored in the repo, the desk or a chat: it lives in `/etc/rynobot/keys/<bot>.json` on the server, readable only by your user; the desk runs as another user and cannot read it. Fund each wallet with no more than that bot is allowed to lose.

**Going live** has its own checklist at the bottom of the desk, ticking off by itself as the dry run goes on: two weeks of dry run, reality checks with enough shared trades, a council bot candidate, one wallet per bot, keys only on the server, the hard limits and kill switches (in place), and the live order code, which is deliberately not built until the data earns it.

Every coin name on the desk links to its DexScreener chart. Click a row for the full record of that trade: before the entry (when the setup was found, how long it waited, the trigger candle, the price when the bot acted and what it paid after costs, pool liquidity then and now, price impact) and since the entry (best and worst price, every stop move, what is banked and what is still held).

It runs as the `rynobot-dash` service under its own user `rynodesk`, reads the bots' files and nothing secret: its environment file `/etc/rynobot-desk.env` holds only the desk password (`DASH_PASS_B64`), the optional control password (`DASH_CONTROL_B64`), `DASH_CONTROLS`, `DASH_BIND` and `DASH_PORT`. The ntfy topic, GitHub token and API keys stay in `/etc/rynobot.env`, which the desk cannot open. Wrong passwords slow down per address and are logged for fail2ban. The full posture, and the checklist before real money, is in **bot/SECURITY.md**.

## What reaches your phone

Only what needs you. The bot trades; you don't, so would-be buys and sells stay on the server (`node bot/run.mjs --status` shows them).

- **"RynoBot started (dry run)"** when it starts or restarts.
- **A problem**, once it has lasted long enough to matter, and a note when it clears by itself:
  - can't get prices for 15 minutes with a position open (30 minutes otherwise)
  - can't get 5-minute candles for 30 minutes (no new entries can trigger)
  - can't read the scanner's setups for 30 minutes
  - 5 checks in a row failed (a bug or a full disk: send me `journalctl -u rynobot@rules -n 50`, or `@breakout`)
- **"RynoBot Breakout: filters changed"** when the tuner switches a filter on or off (rare: it needs new trades on both sides).
- **"RynoBot has gone quiet"**: the bot posts a heartbeat every 30 minutes to a silent second topic (`<your topic>-hb`, which your phone doesn't subscribe to). The GitHub watchdog checks it every 30 minutes and tells you once if it stops for 90 minutes (overnight it waits until 6am), and once when it's back.
- **The reality check**, every week.

To get the would-be trades on your phone again, set `"tradeAlerts": true` in `bot/config.json`; `"dailySummary": true` brings back the 8pm summary.

## The kitty (banked profit, out of harm's way)

The pot stays at $10,000. Whenever cash plus what is still invested in open positions adds up to more than that, the excess is banked profit and is swept to the **kitty** (at least $25 at a time, from cash only, so an open winner is not counted until it is sold). Position size, the daily loss limit and risk in use all look at the pot, never the kitty: a winning streak does not grow the bets, and the kitty is never used to top the pot up after losses. The desk shows pot, kitty and the total; the total is what P&L is measured on.

In dry run and rehearsal the kitty is a paper number. In live mode the same amount of SOL or ETH is **really sent** from the bot's wallet to your kitty wallet: `"kittyWallet"` under that bot in `bot/config.json`, the **public address of your own Phantom** on the bot's chain (Solana address for a Solana bot, 0x address for a Robinhood-chain bot). No key is involved: the bot can only ever send profit to the address you wrote down, and from Phantom you off-ramp as usual. A live bot with no kitty wallet set does not sweep (one phone message asks for the address) so its books and wallet never drift apart. Each sweep keeps the fee reserve in the wallet, goes in the journal with its transaction, and tells your phone. `"kitty": false` switches it off (profit then compounds in the pot).

## The market dial

Every bot scales its risk per trade by the scanner's market regime (from the Grok/QuantGPT desk idea, and what the paper **Sized by market** bot sim tests): SOL risk-off x0.5, neutral x1, risk-on x1.25 (`"dial"` in config; `"regimeDial": false` turns it off). It changes the size only, never the entries, exits or stops, and each buy says which dial it used.

## Reality check (weekly, automatic)

The dashboard's **Bot rules** book replays the same rules on candles. A candle replay sees every high and low, so a target touched for ten seconds counts as sold; the bot only sees the price once a minute, the way you would. Every 7 days the bot compares the two on the coins both took and sends you the result:

- how each side did on the same trades, and the gap between them (with a 90% range)
- where the gap comes from: worse entries, or targets the paper booked that the bot never reached
- every paper trade the bot missed, and why (offline, all slots full, price ran first, the trigger didn't fire, never saw the setup)
- what to do with it: the amount to knock off every paper book's average before trusting it (once there are 10 or more shared trades)

The first one arrives 7 days after each bot starts (it says "too few" until 10 shared trades exist; the gate for real money stays at a fortnight). To see it any time: `node bot/run.mjs --reality` (add `--bot breakout` for that bot, which is compared with the **Breakout → Bot filters** paper book). Each result is also kept in `bot/reality.json` / `bot/reality-breakout.json`. Change the timing with `"realityEveryDays"` in `bot/config.json`.

## Settings (`bot/config.json`)

`accountUsd` paper account size (10,000; a bot that has not traded yet picks up a change on restart, otherwise use `--reset`) · `riskPct` % of the account risked per trade · `maxOpen` positions at once · `maxOpenRiskPct` total open risk · `dailyLossPct` no new entries after this losing day · `minOpp` lowest scanner score to watch · `signals` `["bid", "watch"]` or just `["bid"]` · `pollSec` seconds between checks · `tradeAlerts` would-be trades to the phone (default false) · `dailySummary` the 8pm summary (default false) · `summaryHourBrisbane` its time · `realityEveryDays` days between reality checks (7).

These apply to every bot. To change one bot only, put it under `"bots"`, for example `"bots": { "breakout": { "autoTune": false } }` keeps the Breakout bot on its starting filter. Breakout-only settings: `autoTune` (let the tuner switch filters, default true) · `maxStopPct` (35) · `minOpp` (50) · `maxHoldH` (168).
